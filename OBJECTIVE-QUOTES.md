# The objective, in the owner's words

Jesse Daniel Brown, November 9, 2026, quoted with his permission ("You can quote me on that, Jesse Daniel Brown today").

> I'm trying to prove the illusion of continuous arises from the finite world.

> And it's also that we're in a matrix, so the hashes matter too. Like quantum entanglement as a hash.

> [Icosian] 3 space, the 24-dimensional world, which is laden with exceptions and these groups, but because the entire subject presumes defining representations. You know, when you have, say, SU3, you imagine complex 3 space as the raison d'etre of that group's existence. The problem here is that these are the tiny number of groups for which this paradigm, that is, deprojectivized linear representations, fails. And so by virtue of the pedagogy, you are led into thinking that somehow you're looking at the platypus and the echidna and saying, well, these are just, you know, sort of exception mammals, when in fact they're the most important mammals of all because they predate the marsupial branching on the phylogenetic tree.

The passages are as received in the session transcript. The bracketed word is an editorial reading: the transcript has "Ionic 3 space"; the 24-dimensional object built as 3-space over the icosians is the Leech lattice (Tits, 1980).

How the work tests it: the frozen predictions in this folder state, for each finite subgroup of SU(2) and for the 600-cell, the E8 roots and the Leech minimal vectors, the exact degree up to which the finite set is indistinguishable from the continuous sphere or group, and where it first shows its finiteness. The measurement runs push past each predicted break.
